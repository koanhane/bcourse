Calendar brings classes, due dates, and start reminders into one daily agenda. Inspired by Google and Apple Calendar, it adds class times and locations so coursework can be planned around the day.

Keep the plan current.
Return to the right task.

Adjust → Act

After choosing a start reminder, students still need to know when the plan changes.

Notifications separates Date changed and New assignment updates. “Needs your attention,” bold text, and a highlighted area prioritize unread deadline changes. Old and new dates explain the difference, including the revised reminder time.

Inbox makes those updates easy to find again: All / Unread / Reminders filters, course and type labels, message previews, and unread indicators lead into the relevant message or assignment.

04 / Research

Remembering the deadline
is only part of making a plan.

38 of 49 respondents missed or nearly missed a deadline at least once in the previous six months.

“I forgot in the evening”

“Although I knew in the morning about the deadline, I forgot in the evening to do it and forgot to take the quiz.”Anonymous response · P45

Our response: a reminder at the planned start time, with a direct path back to the assignment. To-do keeps the saved reminder visible; Inbox provides a Reminders filter to find it again.

“I underestimated the amount of work”

“I underestimated the amount of work I still had left, and did not dedicate enough time to this assignment, which coincided with midterms.”Anonymous response · P12

Our response: show estimated effort on Dashboard and To-do, allow a custom duration, and place deadlines and reminders beside classes in Calendar.

These responses informed the design; they do not validate the solution. Calendar’s Google/Apple inspiration and the prioritization of date changes are team design choices, not claims made by these respondents.

Source: Form Responses 1, 49 consented responses, Oct 18–22, 2026. Deadline question M2:M50: 20 once; 12 two–three times; 6 four or more; 11 zero. Quotes: P45 and P12.

07Design Decisions

The choices behind
the connected experience.

  1. Keep setup in one place

    The full sheet groups effort, time, and delivery choices. One-tap setup is faster, and inline setup stays in the list; the full sheet has room for all three choices.

  2. Let the student override the estimate

    V3 keeps the AI explanation but adds Custom. The estimate helps start a plan; it should not decide the plan for the student.

  3. Show the plan after it is saved

    Effort and reminder times stay in the task list. Students can review the plan without reopening every assignment. The tradeoff is more information in each row.

  4. Bring the schedule into Canvas

    Calendar includes classes as well as due dates and reminders, drawing on the daily planning patterns of Google Calendar and Apple Calendar. Category filters let students reduce the visible detail when they only need one kind of event.

  5. Give consequential updates more emphasis

    Notifications separates unread deadline changes from routine updates. The crossed-out old date, bold new date, and adjusted reminder explain what the student needs to reconsider. Inbox adds type and course labels so messages can be identified before they are opened.

08Constraints

This is a prototype,
not a live reminder service.

  • Sample data: course details and AI history are prototype content. Estimate accuracy and live Canvas integration are not established.
  • Research scope: 49 self-reported survey responses describe this sample. They do not measure the feature’s effectiveness.
  • Delivery and date changes: phone/email delivery and automatic reminder adjustments are illustrated with sample data. The HTML does not establish a live connection to Canvas or an email service.
09Reflections

The next test is whether
students can use it on their own.

The design now connects a deadline with effort and a start time. What remains is to check whether students understand that plan without coaching.

  • Test the reminder: can a student set a time, find it again, and change it?
  • Test trust: do students treat the AI estimate as a suggestion and know how to override it?
  • Test the expanded views: can a student find upcoming work from a course card, plan around classes, and identify an earlier deadline from Notifications?
  • Test completion: do students understand that Mark as done does not submit their work?

What we would revise next

After testing, use the mistakes observed in those sessions to decide what to simplify. In particular, check the dense task rows and the wording of completion and default reminders.
