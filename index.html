04Research

38 of 49 students missed
or nearly missed a deadline.

The supplied student survey contains 49 responses, collected October 18–22, 2026. All 49 rows include consent. Counts below use all responses unless otherwise stated.

38 / 49

Missed or nearly missed an assignment deadline at least once in the past six months.20 reported once; 12 reported 2–3 times; 6 reported 4 or more. Source: M2:M50.

37 / 49

Saw a course announcement later than needed at least once in the past month.16 reported once; 15 reported 2–3 times; 6 reported 4 or more. Another 11 reported zero and 1 was unsure. Source: N2:N50.

24 / 49

Selected Google or Apple Calendar as a way to track assignments and deadlines.25 respondents selected the bCourses To Do list. This was a multi-select question; groups can overlap. Source: F2:F50.

“A due date changed but my Notion still had the old date.”Anonymous survey response · Form Responses 1, P19

Our interpretation: visibility and keeping information current deserve attention together. A consolidated mobile task list may help students scan their work, but cannot repair missing or outdated information on its own.

“Lecture slides were under Files in one class and Modules in another.”Anonymous survey response · Form Responses 1, R14

A limit exposed by the responses: reminders cannot fix information that is missing or in the wrong place. The feature addresses when to start an assignment, not course organization. Whether this finding surprised the team still needs their account.

06Prototyping & Testing

V2 clarified the plan.
V3 made it more editable.

The supplied files identify V2 as “after feedback.” The comparisons below show the changes visible in those exports. Feedback wording, participant count, session method, and retest outcomes were not supplied.

The reminder moved from a time choice to a more explicit plan.

V1 selected “Tonight, 8:00 PM.” V2 instead labels a day-before reminder as the default and adds an explanation of the estimated work.

  • Estimate context added: V2 labels the three-hour value as AI-generated and cites the lab’s five questions and a sample average from earlier labs.
  • Default made explicit: “Day before, Wed 8:00 PM (default)” replaces tonight as the selected option.
  • Fallback explained: V2 says the default reminder applies even when this step is skipped.

What still needs checking: whether students understand and accept the automatic reminder. The displayed history is prototype content, not validated prediction data.

The list changed from counting items to showing workload.

V1 says “4 items due this week.” V2 shows about 8.5 hours of work, groups assignments by day, and adds effort and reminder details to each row.

  • Workload surfaced: daily totals make a heavier day visible without opening individual assignments.
  • Reminders surfaced: each row shows its default reminder time.
  • Personal work given an entry point: a plus button appears in the header.

What still needs checking: whether the extra labels help planning or slow scanning. The V2 export also shows the weekly summary wrapping into the first date heading; this visible layout issue should be checked on the target phone.

V2 → V3 / Final iteration

Keep the planning model. Refine the controls and task layout.

V3 is the final mobile design shown in the walkthrough and final mobile screen exports. It retains V2’s explained AI estimate, day-before default, and visible workload. The following changes are visible in the supplied V2 and final exports.

1. Preset duration → preset or custom duration

V2: the Lab 6 sheet offers ~1 hr, ~3 hrs (AI), and A day+. V3: it keeps those presets and adds Custom.

Design implication: a student can enter a duration that does not fit the three presets, while still seeing the AI suggestion. This is a visible capability change, not a measured improvement in estimate accuracy.

2. Large date headings → a date column beside the tasks

V2: assignments sit under large day headings, with daily totals on the right. V3: dates and daily totals move into the left column; course, assignment, due time, effort, and reminder sit beside them. The weekly summary stays below the blue To-do header.

Design implication: date context and task details can be scanned together. The summary and first date heading are separated in the V3 export, addressing the overlap visible in V2. Whether scanning is faster still needs testing.

3. A generic list style → the final Canvas mobile visual treatment

V2: the list uses a large “To Do” title, rounded groups, course-color dots, and a floating plus button. V3: it uses a blue app header with menu, add, and filter controls; assignment rows use course labels, icons, and dividers. The bottom navigation includes task and inbox counts.

Design implication: the reminder feature is presented within a more complete mobile app context. Brand familiarity and discoverability have not been evaluated with participants.

These explanations describe observable changes and their design implications. The team’s original reasons and feedback for the V2→V3 revision still need confirmation. The final recording demonstrates completion and reminder-off states; the supplied V2 exports do not establish when those interactions were first introduced.

07Design Decisions

Keep the setup together.
Bring the plan back to the list.

The selected and rejected forms are documented in V0. The rationales below interpret the visible design and research; they do not substitute for the team’s account of its decisions.

1. A full sheet instead of one tap or inline setup

Chosen: a dedicated space for effort, timing, and delivery. Alternatives dropped: the assignment’s one-tap reminder and the expandable task row.

Design tradeoff: the full sheet accommodates more choices, but requires another screen. The sketch confirms the selection; the team’s actual reason is still to be documented.

2. An explained estimate instead of an unlabeled duration

V1 offers time presets. V2 names the AI estimate and shows its proposed basis. A survey respondent described underestimating work (P12), making effort planning relevant. Explaining an estimate does not establish that it is accurate.

See the reminder comparison ↑

3. Workload and reminder timing instead of an item count alone

V2 adds daily effort totals and reminder details to the task list. These may help distinguish four small tasks from four large ones; the cost is a denser list. This benefit still needs usability evaluation.

See the task-list comparison ↑

4. Custom effort and an integrated task layout in V3

V3 keeps the V2 planning model while adding a custom duration and moving daily dates and totals beside task rows. This gives students more control over the estimate and changes how the list is scanned. The team’s rationale and any usability effect still need confirmation.

08Constraints

Reminders depend on accurate course information.

Course data and integration

This is a prototype with sample course data. Live Canvas syncing, external class tools, reminder delivery, changed-date handling, and AI estimate accuracy have not been verified. The notification and email screens illustrate intended delivery states; they do not establish a working notification service.

Research scope

The survey has 49 self-reported responses. Recruitment details and sample representativeness are unknown. The findings describe this sample.

Process documentation

V0 sketches, V1 screens, V2 exports labeled after feedback, and V3 final screens and recording document the design’s progression. The feedback record, rejection reasons, and retest results are still missing. Project duration, individual roles, and tooling details also need confirmation.

Accessibility and devices

The product concept still needs keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, and mobile evaluation. A recorded mobile walkthrough is insufficient to assess these.

09Reflections

Visibility is a starting point.
Trust is the harder part.

The survey supports a problem worth investigating: students report missing deadlines and seeing updates late. The next step is to learn whether this mobile reminder flow helps them find and act on the right information without guidance.

What we would improve next: test reminder and completion flows, check effort-estimate totals, clarify how changed dates and external assignments are represented, and connect the documented alternatives and revisions to the team’s original feedback notes. V3 separates the summary from the first date heading; we would verify that separation and the denser task rows on the target phone.

AI use in this case study

AI assisted with the case-study website’s writing, layout, and implementation. The team overruled the earlier image-heavy layout and requested clearer typography and a mobile-only focus. The product’s original AI use and any decisions the team overruled there still need to be documented.
